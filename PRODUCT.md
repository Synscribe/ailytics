## Platform

web

## Users

- **Buyers:** heavy-industry and critical-infrastructure stakeholders: safety (EHS) leaders, operations leaders, and security leaders. Innovation teams sometimes source on their behalf. Market order: US, then UK, then Australia.
- **Day-to-day users of the product (who buyers picture while reading):** site supervisors, operations staff and operations managers. Senior management uses the multi-site overview.
- **Pre-demo evaluators:** IT and cyber reviewers (questionnaires, data residency), procurement.
- **Situation:** site monitoring today is manual (watching screens, walkarounds). It does not scale, people tire, and hazards are missed. Triggers include regulator crackdowns after accidents (OSHA, MOM), being in the news for a death or fine, liability evidence, insurance premiums, a recent security incident, and constant interest in productivity.

## Product Purpose

Ailytics turns the CCTV a site already owns into AI video analytics that detects safety, security and productivity risks as they happen, and alerts the right people with evidence. The website's job: in 6 months, inbound inquiries should come from search (SEO) and AI answers (GEO), not only partners and referrals. Success signal: direct inquiries from organic channels.

## Positioning

- Category: AI video analytics (buyers also say vision AI, computer vision, industrial safety).
- First impression: heavy industry and critical infrastructure. Generic "AI video surveillance" is too broad and attracts retail and F&B enquiries.
- Outcomes: Safety, Security, Productivity, on cameras the customer already has.
- Edges over competitors (Intenseye, Invigilo, Protex AI, Spot AI): single-camera 2D to 3D distance, accuracy in harsh conditions (rain, dust, low light), customisable use cases, customer success beyond support, a 24/7 global ops team.
- Two technology stories: detection models (50+ use cases, trained per scenario) and the vision language model AilyssaX ("talk to your cameras": plain-language search and scene reasoning). OK to show the VLM now; the client calls it the future of vision AI.
- The site must read as a US company.

## Operating Context

- Outdoor and industrial sites with existing IP cameras, often low resolution, in poor light and weather.
- Alerts reach supervisors through the mobile app, messaging groups and site radios. Managers review multi-site dashboards and reports.
- Buying cycle: budgets in January and February, plus year-end leftover budget. Pre-demo questions: cyber clearance, accuracy, data residency, camera compatibility, track record in that country or industry. Objections: price, "the problem is too small", "a cheap AI camera would do".

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Industries, tier 1 (homepage and marketing focus): construction, manufacturing (including food, chemical, pharma), oil and gas, logistics and warehousing, maritime and ports, airports and air cargo, mining, energy and plants, data center operations (no live customer yet).
- Industries, tier 2 (own pages, not on the homepage, not pushed): education and campus security, childcare and preschool, elderly care, healthcare, correctional facilities, dormitories, environment and animals.
- Never target: retail stores, F&B, restaurants, hospitality.
- Gun detection: dedicated US landing pages only, never the homepage. Sensitive, especially schools.
- Defense: "open to defense customers, talk to us"; no public use cases.
- Not an EHS system (integrates with EHS platforms), not a camera hardware vendor, not construction progress or project management (progress features are only X-ray comparison, timelapse, soil-truck tracking), no facial recognition or border control, visual QA/QC is not a focus.
- Deployment: edge, on-premise or cloud. Connects over RTSP or ONVIF.
- Trust items to make visible for US/UK/AU: ISO/IEC 27001, face blurring the customer cannot override, local data residency, help with union negotiations, yearly external penetration testing (VAPT), projects in Europe. SOC 2 Type 1 is in progress, not complete.
- No public pricing.
- Navigation decided 13 Oct: Use cases (`/uses`), Industries (`/industries`), Case studies, Blog, Request a demo button. About, Partner with us, Projects and Careers live in the footer. Localisation (Japan, Taiwan, Korea) is a later phase.

## Product Principles

1. Lead with what Ailytics detects on cameras the visitor already owns; explain the technology after the value is clear.
2. Every claim traces to the claims ledger; when proof is missing, show less rather than invent.
3. Heavy industry and critical infrastructure first; never drift toward generic surveillance or retail.
4. Answer the pre-demo questions (accuracy, data, cameras, track record) before the visitor has to ask.
5. Each page has one search purpose and one next action: request a demo.
